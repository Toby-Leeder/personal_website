*Buy Buy Bestie* is a short film I directed with the Business and Film Association (BAFA) at UC Berkeley, with a crew of 20+ and a cast of 4.

Directing it was the first time I was truly in charge of a team. Over twenty people were looking to me, literally, for direction. That made it an incredible learning experience in working with people under pressure, in a stressful environment, for nearly 12-hour days.

## What went well

### Leadership

My leadership style is consensus-driven. I always try to ask others for their input, and on a project like this that mattered more than ever. I had never directed anything before, and the people around me knew far more about directing than I did. I leveraged their knowledge, asking questions constantly to learn as much as I could, while still balancing that against implementing my own ideas.

I carry that mindset into everything I do. Some people will always know more than me, so I learn from them while still bringing my own thoughts to the table.

### Stress

Being on set is constant crunch time. There are always tight deadlines and you are always running late. These are the environments I thrive in. I kept track of our shot list and schedule while overseeing creative decisions for the actors, the cinematography, and the lighting.

### Communication

With 20 people on set, clear lines of communication between my producers, actors, assistant director, and everyone else were essential. I kept a central group chat going, but I also reached out to people directly and often. Instead of leaving them to report back on their own, asking them directly was a friendly way to remind and encourage them to stay in touch without putting too much pressure on anyone.
